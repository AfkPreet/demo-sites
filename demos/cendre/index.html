I — Le feu

There is no gas in this building.

The hearth is lit at five in the morning and it is not allowed to go out. By service it has become a bank of embers three feet deep, and everything that reaches your table has passed through some part of it — the flame, the coals, the ash underneath, or the smoke above.

Vine cuttings for the birds. Old oak for the roots. Fig wood, once a year, for one week, when there is fig wood.

II — Les services

Four of the twelve, plated in front of you.

II

Céleri

Ash-baked celeriac, hazelnut, four-year vinegar

Buried in the embers at four in the afternoon and dug out at seven. The skin is charcoal. The inside is custard.

III

Langoustine

Over embers, fennel, burnt lemon

Ninety seconds on the bars, split, and back on for eleven more. Anything longer and you are eating a memory of it.

VII

Pigeon

Cherry, smoked marrow, elderberry

Hung eight days. Cooked on the bone over vine cuttings, rested longer than it cooked, and carved at the pass while you watch.

XI

Miel brûlé

Burnt honey, sheep’s milk, pine

The honey is taken to the exact second before it turns bitter, which is a decision, not a temperature.

    III — La salle

    Eighteen people, once a night.

    Everyone sits at seven thirty. Everyone eats the same twelve things in the same order. There is no à la carte, there are no substitutions, and there is exactly one table that can see all the way into the fire — which we give to whoever books last, because that seems fairer.

    The forge was working until 1974. The anvil is still bolted to the floor by the pass and we have never once thought about moving it.

    Réserver
