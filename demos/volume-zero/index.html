02 — Selected work

Eleven built. Six worth showing.

  1. A workshop and a bed, sharing one chimney. The furnace is the only heating.

  2. A 1931 tram depot, kept whole. We added stairs, glass and a lavatory block, and removed nothing.

  3. Two rammed-earth walls, dug from the plot itself, and a roof laid across them. Nothing else.

  4. A chapel built entirely from the offcuts of a quarry twelve kilometres away. It seats nineteen.

  5. Eighteen dwellings arranged around a shared glasshouse that is warm enough to sit in for nine months of the year.

  6. A 1958 grain silo becoming a library. The concrete stays; we are only cutting windows and putting a stair inside.

03 — How we work

Four rules, and we keep them

  1. 01

    The greenest building is the one already standing

    We ask first whether the thing needs building at all. Four of our last six commissions turned into repairs, and two of them turned into nothing, which we count as a result.

  2. 02

    Draw it at 1:20, or don't draw it

    Anything that cannot survive being drawn at 1:20 will not survive being built. Every junction in every project is drawn at full detail before it is priced.

  3. 03

    One material, doing three jobs

    A brick wall that is structure, finish and thermal mass costs less carbon than three layers doing one job each — and it is repairable by someone who is not us.

  4. 04

    Finish, then stop

    The last ten per cent of a building is where architects usually add the things nobody asked for. We hand over and go away.

04 — The studio

Eleven people and a crane we may not move

Volume Zero began in 2014 with two people, one commission and a shared conviction that Denmark did not need any more new buildings than it could help. We work out of a former shipyard shed on Refshaleøen, under a 1960s gantry crane that is listed and therefore permanent.

We take three or four projects a year. We draw everything ourselves, we visit every site every week it is open, and we would rather lose a competition than win it with a rendering.
