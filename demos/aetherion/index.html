01 · Leaving

Everyone remembers the first time the horizon curves.

You board at Kourou at 04:10. Ninety-one minutes later the sky behind you stops being blue and simply stops. We give passengers no schedule for this part of the flight. In nineteen crossings nobody has looked away.

02 · The long dark

Nine weeks with nothing outside the window.

There is no scenery between the planets. There is a sun the size of a coin, a silence that turns out to have texture, and a hundred and six people finding out what they actually think about. Our crews call this stretch the quiet freight.

Point six of a gravity.

The habitat ring turns once every eleven seconds. It is not enough to fool physics and it is entirely enough to fool your inner ear, which is the part of you that files the complaints.

03 · The ring plane

On day sixty-one, something ahead of you catches the light.

The plane is nine hundred metres thick and four hundred thousand kilometres across. From above it is a sheet of ice. From inside it is weather.

Ring plane 0.0 km above

You cross it in eleven seconds.

And then you are through.

Every passenger who has done it describes the same thing, in the same order: the noise of the hull, the light going sideways, and then a quiet so complete that people apologise for breathing.

04 · Daylight

The far side is lit.

Sunlight comes back off four hundred thousand kilometres of ice in every direction at once. For four days there is no night anywhere on the ship. The crew stops calling it a shift and starts calling it a watch.
