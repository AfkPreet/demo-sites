01 — Why it matters

A visitor decides how much to trust you in about two seconds. Everything I do lives inside those two seconds.

Most websites are brochures with a nicer font. They present information and hope. The ones that convert do something else entirely — they move, they reveal, they reward the scroll, and by the time someone reaches your contact form they already feel like they know how you work.

That feeling is not decoration. It is the product. It is built out of timing, easing curves, restraint, and an obsession with the frame budget — because beauty that stutters reads as broken, and broken reads as amateur.

02 — Who you'd be working with

I'm Preet. I design the thing, then I build the thing.

No handoff, no telephone game between a designer's file and a developer's approximation of it. The motion you approve is the motion that ships, because the person choosing the easing curve is the person writing it.

I work in the narrow band where art direction meets engineering: WebGL and shaders, scroll choreography, type systems, and the deeply unglamorous performance work that makes all of it feel effortless on a four-year-old Android.

04 — How it goes

Four steps. No mystery, no drift.

  1. 01

    A 30-minute call

    You tell me what the site has to do — the client it has to win, the objection it has to answer. I tell you straight whether I'm the right person for it.

  2. 02

    Direction, not mockups

    Within a week you get one art direction, one motion study, and the actual hero built in the browser. You approve the feeling before anyone builds a page.

  3. 03

    Build in the open

    A live staging link from day one, updated as I go. You watch it come together on your own phone rather than in a slide deck.

  4. 04

    Ship, measure, hand over

    Deployed, fast, documented, and yours — with the CMS or the codebase set up so your team can keep moving without me.
